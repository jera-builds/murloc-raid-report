How we measure (and why)

Every number on this page is here to answer one question: how do you stack up against the population of people actually raiding right now — not just against the 23 other names on this roster. Here's what each metric means, in plain terms, and why we bothered tracking it.

Parse percentile

Line up 100 players of your exact spec who killed this boss this week. Your parse is how many of them you'd beat on a typical pull — a parse of 90 means you outperformed 90 of the 100, a parse of 50 means you're dead in the middle of the pack. We use percentile instead of raw DPS or HPS because raw numbers aren't fair to compare: gear, boss health, and fight length all change what a "big number" even looks like from week to week. Percentile strips all of that out and leaves you with one honest question — where do you sit against everyone else playing your character, right now, on this boss.

Best vs. median

One great pull can be luck — every cooldown lined up, the tank never blinked, RNG smiled on you. The median is the pull you'd bet on if we ran it again tomorrow: your typical night, not your five best minutes. We track median, not best, because a raid team survives on typical output week after week, not on one highlight-reel parse. Week 1 is a special case: every boss has exactly one kill logged, so best and median are mathematically the same number this week — the gap becomes real (and worth watching) once repeat kills stack up in week 2.

Blended median & the 40/60 split

Normal and Heroic tell different stories this early in the tier. Normal is mechanically easy, so it's the cleanest read on your raw rotation; Heroic is still fresh enough that everyone's parses run low while the group is still learning the fight. We blend them 40% Heroic / 60% Normal so one shaky prog pull can't sink your week, and one clean Normal clear can't paper over a rough Heroic pull. That split isn't fixed — it climbs toward Heroic every week the guild banks more Heroic kills, the same way Season 1's blend leaned harder on Mythic once there was enough Mythic sample to trust.

THE BAR = 75

Why 75 and not 50? Because the average logged player isn't the average player in the game — logging your kills to Warcraft Logs is opt-in, and the people who bother tend to already be trying harder than the players who don't. That pulls the real midpoint of the logging population above 50, so a 50 bar would reward mediocrity, not competence. Why not 95? Because 95 describes a best-in-world night, not a standard a 24-person roster can hit every single week without burning out — that's a ceiling, not a bar. 75 is where a solidly-played, well-prepared raider lands on a normal Tuesday: high enough to mean something, realistic enough to be a weekly habit instead of a lottery ticket.

Pull attendance

Of the 39 times we pulled a boss this week, how many were you standing in the room for? Practice reps are the raid's paycheck — every pull, the group re-learns a fight's timing a little better, and if you weren't there, the raid learned it without you and you're catching up on your own dime next time. We count it as a straight share (your pulls ÷ 39) because attendance doesn't care how good the pull was, only whether you banked the rep.

Provisional GM score (70/30)

The GM score is one number meant to answer "how's this player doing overall" at a glance — this week it's 70% blended median percentile plus 30% pull attendance. That's a provisional formula: the full Season-1-style score also weighs death count, potion usage and mechanic killing-blows, but our pulls of Warcraft Logs' deaths, casts and buffs tables failed during the week-1 refresh (a route-format change on WCL's side, since diagnosed — the official-API pipeline replaces those pulls). Rather than guess at numbers we couldn't verify, we dropped those three components for one week and reweighted what's left. The full five-part formula returns the moment the API is back — expected by the next refresh.

Status chips

Above bar / Near bar / Below bar are the same blended-median number sorted into three buckets: 75+ is Above, 60–74 is Near, under 60 is Below. We split it this way because a single number is hard to scan across 24 rows — the chip lets you spot who needs a look without doing the arithmetic yourself. "Near bar" specifically means coachable: close enough that a rotation fix or a gear upgrade closes the gap, not a fundamental rework.

Guild rank — World / US / Realm

World 2,287 · US 988 · Realm 73 are the same rank at three different zoom levels — how the guild stacks up against every Heroic-progressed guild on the planet, in the US region, and on our own realm. It's a race result: "73rd" means nothing until you know the size of the field. We pull all three because a guild can look great on a small realm and be entirely unremarkable worldwide — the wider numbers keep the local number honest.

"Boss at X%" — the wipe convention

When a pull doesn't end in a kill, we record it as "boss at X%" — the percentage of the boss's health bar still standing when the last raider died or the pull was called. Lower is better: "boss at 45%" means the raid burned the boss down past half health before the wipe, which is deeper progress than "boss at 80%." It's the same convention Warcraft Logs itself uses on the report page, so the number here always matches what you'd see opening the log yourself.

Why week 1 is a "first sounding"

A sounding is the first depth reading a ship takes in unfamiliar water — useful for orienting yourself, useless as a final measurement. Three things make week 1 specifically unstable: every boss has exactly one kill logged, so there's no repeat data to smooth out a lucky or unlucky pull; Warcraft Logs only locks ranks in permanently at noon UTC each day, so a percentile you saw Thursday night can still drift before it locks; and gear was still landing from Tuesday's loot, so a mid-week ilvl jump changes what "normal" output even looks like for that player. None of that makes the numbers wrong — it means read them as a starting line, not a final grade.
